Geometric analysis: mean curvature flow and minimal surfaces

Talk at the Joint Mathematics Meetings, 2026 · Papers in Parabola, 2025 and 2026

Email: sawyerjacobson@college.harvard.edu

Google Scholar ORCID LinkedIn CV

I study how surfaces evolve under geometric flows, chiefly mean curvature flow: when they settle into minimal surfaces, how singularities form, and which self-similar shapes appear near them. My work pairs maximum-principle arguments for graphical problems with numerical computation of the flow itself. I also enjoy classical convex geometry, where an old theorem often has a cleaner proof waiting for the right point of view.

Research

Uniqueness of the Dirichlet problem for graphical self-shrinkers, preprint (2026) [pdf] [figure]
A direct uniqueness result for graphical self-shrinkers, obtained by linearizing the governing quasilinear elliptic equation and applying a maximum principle.
Approximating graphical minimal surfaces through mean curvature flow (with Jeffery Zhang) [pdf] [journal] [figure] · Parabola 62, no. 1 (2026)
A computational framework for evolving graphical surfaces toward minimality, with simulations testing planarity, rotational symmetry, and complex boundary data.
On graphical minimal surfaces and the homotheticity of singular behavior in mean curvature flow, Joint Mathematics Meetings (2026) [pdf] [slides] [figure]
A self-contained path from graphical minimal-surface theory to computation by mean curvature flow, followed by a study of self-similar models for Type I singularities.
On convex kinematics: a resurrection of Holditch’s theorem (with William Balz) [pdf] [journal] [figure] · Parabola 61, no. 2 (2025)
An intuitive proof of Holditch’s classical area invariant, built from concrete geometry and the complementary perspectives of Green’s and Stewart’s theorems.

Other cool artifacts

The Essence of Algebra: Groups, Linear Algebra, and Representations, lecture notes (2026) Based on Math 55a at Harvard, taught by Joe Harris
A book-length account of Harvard’s honors first-year algebra course, in 27 chapters: groups and their quotients, symmetric and free groups, linear algebra over an arbitrary field through Jordan form and the spectral theorem, multilinear algebra, group actions and the Sylow theorems, and the representation theory of finite groups through induced and real representations. The exercises come from the course’s weekly problem sets, with my solutions in the appendix, and a note at the front describes what taking Math 55 is like.
